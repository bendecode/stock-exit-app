# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The primary user is an individual investor using the app on both desktop and iPhone to monitor existing stock positions. The user checks the app during market hours and after updates to understand whether a holding is approaching a moving stop-profit exit point.

Future users may be general retail investors who want a lightweight exit-monitoring tool without the complexity of a full brokerage terminal.

## Product Purpose

This app tracks stock positions, calculates moving stop-profit thresholds, syncs portfolio data across devices, and highlights when a position may need an exit decision.

Success means the user can open the app and answer three questions quickly:

- Which positions need attention now?
- What is the current profit or loss?
- What price or pullback condition would trigger an exit?

## Brand Personality

Professional, calm, decisive.

The interface should feel closer to a polished brokerage or portfolio monitoring app than a hobby spreadsheet. It should be information-dense without feeling cramped, direct without feeling harsh, and trustworthy without decorative finance clichés.

## Anti-references

Avoid cute, playful, decorative, or gamified finance UI. Avoid large marketing-style hero layouts, oversized cards, excessive empty space, and layouts that force mobile users to scroll before seeing useful position data.

Avoid an engineer-tool look: raw forms, generic gray panels, weak visual hierarchy, and unclear status language. Avoid dark-only trading-terminal aesthetics unless the workflow later calls for a dedicated market-hours mode.

## Design Principles

- Prioritize decision speed: the first screen should show status, risk, profit/loss, and next action without extra explanation.
- Keep controls close to the data they affect: editing a position should feel like adjusting a live monitoring row, not filling out a separate form.
- Make hierarchy obvious: portfolio summary, trend, position row, and details must have distinct visual layers.
- Respect mobile attention: dense but readable layouts, large enough touch targets, and no unnecessary panels before the position list.
- Use finance color carefully: red for gains and attention in the Taiwan market convention, green for losses, yellow for pullback warnings.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for text and controls. Do not rely on color alone for exit, hold, warning, or loss states. Support reduced motion. Keep typography legible on iPhone screens and avoid text overflow in compact position rows.
